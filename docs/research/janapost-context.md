Research date: 17 October 2026. Public company pages and the organizer material supplied by the user are separate sources. The following is product research, not a claim of access to Jana Post's internal systems.

## Product consequences we choose

These are design inferences, not discovered company policies.

**An employee needs context before an action.** A logistics employee opening the portal should see a pending request, an assigned course or an answer that points to a source. The next frontend agent should test this hypothesis with representative users rather than assume that an attractive dashboard proves it.

**Training needs different work contexts.** Synthetic course examples can cover onboarding, customer handovers, package handling, information protection, escalation and supervisor practice. Material must be original, clearly demonstration content and limited to what it actually teaches. An invented instruction must not be described as an official Jana Post safety standard.

**An organization boundary matters before visual hierarchy.** The backend uses legal-entity and team scope. The demo contains another entity and another manager's team so that a permission failure can be demonstrated. Adding partner organizations later requires a separate identity and knowledge-audience decision; partner status does not grant employee profile access.

**Reliability should be visible.** A submitted request keeps its revision and decision history. A repeated click has one effect. If an external export fails, the approval remains recorded and delivery can be retried. The screen should explain which part succeeded and what still needs attention.

**AI answers need accountable content.** A citation identifies a published revision and an accessible excerpt. A missing answer becomes an explicit HR knowledge case only when the employee chooses to escalate. This creates a useful content-maintenance path without delegating employment decisions to a model.

## Boundaries the research cannot settle

No inspected public source establishes Jana Post's actual manager/HR approval order, vacation day-count convention, advance limits, payroll reconciliation rules, document retention rules, production 1C schema, Documentolog API contract or employee knowledge audiences.

The current implementation uses named synthetic policies and explicit adapters. It does not infer employment law from examples. Production decisions require the actual owner of each source and process.

## Focused questions for the next grill-me session

These questions do not block the authorized synthetic implementation. They are the first useful questions when an organizer or company representative becomes available.

1. Who owns the final decision on leave and salary advance, and who substitutes when that person is absent?
2. Does the 1C balance already include approved or exported portal requests, and which identifier lets us reconcile them?
3. Does a manager see direct reports only, a whole department, or a configurable reporting hierarchy?
4. Which employees and partner workers belong in the same portal, and which data must remain separate?
5. Which policy documents can be shared with employees, managers, HR and external partners?
6. What makes a course mandatory, when does it expire, and does a new revision require reassignment?
7. Which one real employee problem would demonstrate business value most clearly in the final presentation?

Answers should update the decision log and the affected policy version. They should not silently rewrite existing submitted evidence.

## Research limits

Public page inspection did not use an internal employee account or submit any company form. Dynamic public page content can be incomplete in text extraction. A missing list of videos or vacancies in that extraction is not evidence that the company has none.
