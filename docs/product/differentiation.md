# Product differentiation through evidence

## The question we can answer

We do not know what other hackathon teams will ship. We cannot honestly claim uniqueness or guarantee a win.
We can choose a product standard, derive it from strong examples and demonstrate that our implementation meets it.

## Selected proof: one changed rule reaches the right people

HR publishes a revised instruction with one practical question. JanaMen assigns it using the policy's existing audience, legal-entity and department scope. Each recipient sees the changed sections as “before / now,” answers a server-scored question and can retry after a wrong answer. Managers see team completion; HR sees their legal entity. Revisions supersede earlier pending assignments, and metadata-only edits do not create a training assignment.

This flow joins the existing policy-version, learning-assignment and notification foundations. It is a concrete differentiator to demonstrate, not a claim that competing teams or commercial products lack similar features. Browser and deployed-environment proof remains open until it is run.

Our proposed standard is that a person can understand what needs attention, why, who acts next and what evidence supports the result.
This standard connects the required modules. It does not depend on a decorative AI assistant or a new slogan.

## Proposed advantages to prove

| Product promise | What the user sees | Backend proof | Design proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every action has a current reason | Pending approval or required course with owner and deadline | Scoped task facts, allowed actions, stable status | User can identify the next action without opening every module |
| Human decisions are traceable | Submitted data, decision, explanation and history | Immutable revisions, actor, version checks, event transaction | Detail view makes the current result and previous decisions readable |
| Official data is distinguishable from prediction | Source balance, freshness and projected leave effect | Imported snapshot separated from portal reservations | Labels prevent a forecast being mistaken for an official balance |
| Learning works at catalogue scale | Assigned, started, passed, failed and overdue learning | Versioned enrollment and attempts, server scoring | Search and filters work with 30 meaningful courses and mixed states |
| AI answers can be inspected | Exact source, version and an honest no-answer state | Scoped retrieval, real chunk ids, citation resolution and evaluation cases | Opening a source preserves the question context and makes evidence findable |
| Missing knowledge reaches an owner | An internal case with progress after explicit escalation | Persisted scoped case, owner and resolution | Employee can tell whether HR has responded |
| Failures do not erase progress | Draft or completed action remains intelligible after a retry | Idempotency, stale-version conflict, outbox retry | Recovery preserves entered information and explains the next step |

These are hypotheses about a stronger demonstration. Validate them with the implementation and browser tasks.
Do not claim professional competitors lack these capabilities. The research shows how established products solve related problems.

## A demonstration with evidence

1. Use an employee with multiple obligations and a populated learning history.
2. Show a leave request with a visible source balance, projected impact and review history.
3. Switch to its assigned manager and show the actual review action.
4. Attempt the same action under an unrelated manager and show server denial.
5. Return to the employee and show the persisted result and notification.
6. Complete a course attempt and inspect the preserved learning result.
7. Ask a supported policy question, open the exact source, then ask an unsupported question.
8. Escalate the unsupported question and open the resulting HR case.
9. Inspect export status separately from business approval.

The value of the sequence is that independent modules agree on the same employee and events.
The browser implementation must make that agreement understandable without exposing internal engineering terms in ordinary employee screens.
